#### F3

`tol K A = ((1+rho)^T - 1)*A + T*(1+rho)^T*alpha`, with `T = cdiv(K, 64)`. Computed exactly, `(1+rho)^T - 1` is `1.259e-4` at K = 512, `1.0076e-3` at K = 4096 and `1.6244e-2` at K = 2^16. The README's numbers are right. Over the host's real use (K <= 4096) the bound means something: with randn inputs, `A ~ 2K/pi` and `|S| ~ sqrt(K)`, so `tol ~ 2.6` against `|S| ~ 64` at K = 4096 (4%). At K = 2^16, though, `tol ~ 678` against `|S| ~ 256`, so for cancelling data the claim does not even fix the sign of an output there. The bound is still the usual worst-case form relative to `sum|a*b|`, it rejects dropped or mis-indexed products (put all the weight on one product), and K = 2^16 is past the host's use. So this is not blocking. The README says 'keeps the tolerance meaningful (1.6% of sum|a*b| at 2^16)'; one sentence noting that for sign-cancelling data near K = 2^16 the bound can exceed |S| would keep downstream readers from reading more into it.

*Evidence:* work/Cover.lean (tol at K = 512 and 4096 checked by norm_num); exact values at K = 2^16 computed with Python fractions

Evaluated where the conditions end, this bound stops telling the kernel apart from plausible wrong ones (REVIEWER_GUIDE section 3: 'a rounding to a lower precision'). (1) Rounding to bfloat16 has relative error up to u/(1+u), with u = 2^-8, about 0.389%. The relative part of tol, (1+rho)^T - 1, passes that at T = 247 steps (K >= 15745) and reaches 1.62% at K = 2^16 (T = 1024). Then |v' - S| <= 0.389%*|S| + e <= 0.389%*A + e, with e the kernel's accumulation error. That is within tol whenever e stays below tol - 0.389%*A: about 1.2% of A at K = 2^16, where an accumulation at the 0.13 of the library's bound that num.md reports for the device is about 0.2% of A. Allowing for that measured error, the crossover is about K = 18177. Rounding both operands to bfloat16 before the dot (about 0.78%) passes from about K = 31681 with an exact accumulation, or about 36417 with the measured one. At the host's own largest K = 4096, tol is 0.10% of A, so Post rejects both. (2) For the script's own input distribution (the benchmark's torch.randn), A is about (2/pi)*K, while the exact result S is typically of size sqrt(K). tol overtakes sqrt(K) from K = 34368 on. At K = 2^16, tol is about 678 against a typical |S| of about 256, so c = 0, or a value of either sign, meets Post for a typical element. The guarantee that holds there (within 1.6% of sum|a*b|) is true and stated, but the K_le docstring and the README present it as keeping the tolerance meaningful, and at the top of the covered range it does not separate a float16 result from a bfloat16 one.
